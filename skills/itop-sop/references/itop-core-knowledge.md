## What iTOP Is

iTOP — Individually Trained Oral Prophylaxis — is a contemporary, evidence-informed approach to oral healthcare designed to strengthen prevention, support therapy, and sustain long-term maintenance. It is not a product training programme and is not a collection of techniques. It is a clinical and educational system for developing sustainable oral health behaviours.

**The founding premise:** In contemporary oral healthcare, the central challenge is no longer a lack of knowledge, clinical skill, or available tools. The gap is between what is known clinically and what is maintained in practice. iTOP finds its relevance precisely within that gap.

**The critical position (2025 update):** iTOP cannot continue to rely on claims that are not scientifically grounded, nor can it be sustained as a system based on rigid techniques or dogmatic rules. In modern healthcare, effectiveness is defined not by how precisely a technique is demonstrated in the clinic, but by whether it can be performed consistently by patients in their daily lives.

---

## The Three Core Criteria

Every technique, tool, and intervention in iTOP must satisfy all three simultaneously:

| Criterion | Clinical meaning | Why it matters |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Acceptance** | The patient accepts the method into their daily routine | If not acceptable, it will not be performed |
| **Atraumaticity** | The technique causes no damage to gums or dental tissue | If it causes harm or discomfort, it will not be repeated |
| **Efficacy** | Biofilm is actually removed when done correctly | If ineffective, the behaviour is pointless regardless of compliance |

These are not abstract values. They are practical clinical filters for every decision about how to select and teach an oral hygiene intervention. Any approach that replaces these principles with rigid protocols risks becoming ineffective regardless of its technical correctness.

## The Knowledge–Behaviour Gap

iTOP's entire educational rationale rests on one empirical observation: **knowledge alone does not produce sustained behaviour**.

Traditional preventive instruction has operated on an implicit logic:
- If professionals understand the correct technique, they will teach it
- If patients receive the right information, they will apply it

Behavioural science and motor learning evidence clearly contradict both assumptions. Performance during instruction is not evidence of learning. Knowledge does not automatically translate into action. Between knowing and doing lies a more complex terrain shaped by habit, context, perception, motor skill, and time.

**Factors that affect whether a patient maintains a behaviour — beyond technique:**
- Time constraints and routine compatibility
- Discomfort and sensory experience
- Cost and accessibility
- Cognitive load in daily life
- Weak or absent follow-up
- Competing priorities

**Consequence for clinical teaching:** The clinician's role is not to instruct — it is to create the conditions in which patients can learn, adapt, and sustain behaviour over time. The clinician becomes a coach and designer of behavioural systems, not a transmitter of information.

## The iTOP Learning Pathway

iTOP is structured as a progressive professional development pathway. Each level builds on the previous one.

### iTOP Info
- Single lecture, up to 90 minutes
- Introduction to iTOP methods and philosophy only
- **Does NOT count as an official seminar** — cannot replace any formal level
- Can be run by a Lecturer or Instructor

### iTOP Introductory
**Theme:** Starting the Journey — Rethinking Prevention

**Format:**
- 1 day, blended learning seminar
- Instructor-to-participant ratio: **max 1:6 for T2T**
- Target audience: dental professionals attending their first iTOP seminar
- Combines theoretical input and practical Touch to Teach (T2T) training

**Educational logic — three stages:**
1. **Reframing** — participants are invited to reconsider why traditional preventive instruction often fails
2. **Experiencing** — participants engage directly in T2T practice to feel the demands of skill acquisition, precision, and adaptation
3. **Integrating** — the practical experience connects back to clinical reasoning and sustainable patient support

**Standard agenda:**
| Time | Content |
|------|---------|
| Introduction | 30 min — Programme orientation |
| Theory 1 | 45 min + 15 min discussion — Controlling Oral Biofilm: The Missing Link between Knowledge and Action |
| T2T 1 | 90 min — Touch to Teach: Interproximal Biofilm Control (IDB selection, force calibration, individual adaptation) |
| Lunch | Break |
| T2T 2 | 60 min — Touch to Teach: Toothbrushing Techniques (manual and powered, precision, adaptation) |
| Theory 2 | 45 min + 15 min discussion — From Technique to Behaviour: Building Sustainable Oral Hygiene |

**Learning objectives:** Participants completing the Introductory seminar should be able to:
- Understand the rationale and core philosophy of iTOP
- Explain why knowledge alone is insufficient to sustain oral health behaviour
- Recognise the importance of the three criteria in individualised biofilm control
- Begin to apply Touch to Teach as a practical training approach
- Support patients through more personalised, behaviour-oriented guidance
- Understand the introductory level as the foundation of a broader iTOP learning pathway

**What the Introductory is NOT:** It does not aim to produce mastery. Its function is to reorient professional thinking, introduce a shared conceptual language, and provide a first structured experience of the iTOP system.

---

### iTOP Advanced
**Theme:** From Technique to Mastery — Leading the Future of Individual Prophylaxis

**Format:**
- 2 days, blended learning seminar
- Instructor-to-participant ratio: **max 1:6 for T2T**; includes 1:1 time with instructor
- Minimum staff: at least 2 iTOP Lecturers
- Participants instruct each other under supervisor

**Three interconnected pillars of the Advanced programme:**
1. **Systemic context** — broadening the clinical view beyond the mouth, placing oral health within wider systemic and environmental contexts
2. **Time and chronicity** — introducing time as an essential factor, stressing that chronic conditions require ongoing management rather than one-off treatments
3. **Motor skill science** — exploring how skills are practised, not just explained

**Full programme structure:**

**Day 1 — From Body to Behaviour**
| Time | Content |
|------|---------|
| Morning | Welcome & Introduction (30 min) |
| | Lecture 1 — Health Factors & Systemic Connection (45 min) |
| | Touch to Teach 1 — Interproximal Biofilm Control (90 min) |
| Afternoon | Touch to Teach 2 — Toothbrushing Techniques (60 min) |
| | Active Learning — Jenga Clinical Tower: Managing Chronic Conditions (90 min) |
| | Lecture 2 — Managing Chronic Diseases: From Science to Strategy (45 min) |

**Day 2 — From Training to Impact**
| Time | Content |
|------|---------|
| Morning | Lecture 3 — Science of Training Motor Skills (45 min) |
| | Touch to Teach 3 — Advanced Tools & Adaptation (90 min) |
| Afternoon | Active Learning — Role-Playing: Patient Interaction & Behaviour (90 min) |
| | Lecture 4 — From Protocol to Patient Impact (45 min) |

**What Advanced is NOT:** Advanced iTOP is not simply "more technique." It is a shift toward mastery, and mastery includes the ability to create learning in others. The aim is not to enhance technical competence alone but to enable participants to translate knowledge into practice in complex, real-world situations.

**Closing perspective:** The day 2 afternoon should be shorter and more active-reflective in format. Emphasis shifts from dense content delivery to consolidation, case discussion, reflection, teaching awareness, and translation into future practice.

## Touch to Teach (T2T)

T2T is one of the pillars of iTOP. It is not simply a hands-on component of the seminar — it is the defining educational strategy through which iTOP translates its scientific and clinical principles into learnable practice.

**Core assumption:** Effective biofilm control cannot be created through explanation alone. It must be developed through guided experience, calibrated feedback, and progressive refinement.

**Dual function within the iTOP pathway:**

| Level | T2T function |
|-------|-------------|
| Introductory | Participants experience and learn biofilm control firsthand — feeling the demands of skill acquisition in their own hands |
| Advanced | T2T evolves into a pedagogical and clinical teaching tool — participants learn how to guide patients through the learning process |

**Why T2T matters beyond technique:** T2T represents the transition from being a professional who gives instructions to being a professional who can coach change. A participant may know the correct technique and still be unable to help a patient integrate it into daily life. T2T addresses this gap.

**The instructor's role in T2T:** The instructor does not simply demonstrate the "right" movement. The instructor structures the learning conditions so that the participant can discover, refine, and eventually reproduce the movement with increasing independence. T2T therefore teaches through content and modelling simultaneously.

## Active Learning Formats

### Jenga Clinical Tower (Day 1 Advanced — 90 min)
**Purpose:** Helps participants understand why managing chronic oral health conditions becomes increasingly difficult over time and why behaviour often breaks down later, even when the initial teaching was technically correct.

**Six categories represented in the tower:** Clinical Knowledge & Technique, Patient Motivation & Attitudes, Communication & Therapeutic Alliance, Life Load & Competing Demands, Physical & Sensory Experience, Follow-Up & Continuity of Care

**Structure:**
- Introduction and setup: 10 min
- Build tower: 10 min
- Debrief on build: 5 min
- Round 1 (Appointment A — inside the clinic): 15 min, each participant rolls D20 once
- Debrief after Round 1: 10 min
- Round 2 (Home / Life — between appointments): 15 min, each participant rolls D20 twice
- Final debrief: 20 min
- Closing message: 5 min

**Key insight:** The problem is rarely knowledge alone. It is how behaviour endures over time under real-life pressure. The tower makes fragility visible.

---

### Role-Play: Patient Interaction & Behaviour (Day 2 Advanced — 90 min)
**Purpose:** Helps participants experience how communication, attitude, listening, and professional positioning influence patient understanding and behaviour. A correct idea can still fail if delivered the wrong way.

**Format:** Two-circle speed consultation
- Inner circle: professionals (fixed)
- Outer circle: patients (rotate after each 1-minute consultation)
- Bell/buzzer marks start, end, and rotation

**Materials:**
- Each professional receives 2 cards: (1) experience/specialty/professional status card; (2) attitude card
- Each patient receives: 1 oral disease/problem card + 6 colour stickers to award to the professional character that helped them most

**Timing:**
- Introduction and briefing: 15 min
- Individual preparation in role: 5 min
- Role-play rounds: 20 min
- Sticker voting: 5 min
- Small-group reflection: 10 min
- Full-group debrief: 30 min
- Final take-home message: 5 min

**Facilitator note:** "We are not evaluating the person. We are not evaluating acting talent. We are evaluating the character and the arguments." This must be repeated clearly before the activity starts.

## iTOP's Evolving Position

iTOP has reached a pivotal point in its development. After nearly twenty years of global adoption, its influence on maintaining patient behaviour and long-term oral health outcomes remains below its full potential. The reason is not a lack of knowledge or tools — it is the continued dominance of a technique-focused, instruction-led approach.

**What must change:**
- From technique demonstration to behaviour design
- From information transfer to coaching and support
- From standardised protocols to adaptive, evidence-based decision-making
- From dogmatic rules to the three criteria as living clinical filters

**Audience segmentation for change management:**
- Early adopters (10–20%): build with them, turn them into lighthouse practices
- Pragmatists (50–60%): show outcomes, simplicity, economic benefit
- Traditionalists (20–30%): do not fight — let evidence and culture shift marginalise dogmatic approaches

**The core question:** Is iTOP a tradition to preserve, or a system to evolve?
