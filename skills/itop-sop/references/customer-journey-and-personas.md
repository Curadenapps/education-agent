# Customer Journey & Curaden Personas

Source: Curaden Customer Journey Guideline Manual — Dental Professionals (v1.0, November 2025), Curaden Personas Deck, Practical Customer Journey Team Guide, Customer Journey Phases (v1.1, 2021)
Confidential — internal Curaden use only.

## Overview

Curaden defines 8 fixed B2B dental professional personas. Their representation may vary by market (some markets have 6 active personas). Personas should be assessed individually, grouped into clusters, and prioritised by value × volume for each market. Update persona mapping annually or as market trends change.

## The 8 Dental Professional Personas

### Persona 1: The Sale Hater
**Profile:** Dr. Max Jamson, 42, Periodontist — runs own clinic with 4 employees

**Defining quote:** "I sell health, not products."

**Key traits:** Passionate about techniques and new approaches; strongly values professional independence; frustrated by sales pressure from reps; well connected regionally and nationally; always up to date with developments

**Core challenge:** Believes in Curaprox products but refuses to sell them in his clinic. Patients end up buying cheaper products in stores. Lacks patient-facing materials to support purchase decisions after the appointment.

**What he wants from Curaden:** Support for convincing patients without requiring in-clinic product sales; better educational materials patients can act on after leaving; technical expertise, not sales pressure

**iTOP relevance:** High. Motivated by the educational, coaching model. Likely to become a strong advocate when treated as a clinical partner, not a sales target.

---

### Persona 2: The Business Woman
**Profile:** Dr. Alice Silva, 37, General dentist — runs small clinic, prevention-focused from the start

**Defining quote:** "Education is an investment we have to spend time and money on."

**Key traits:** Energetic, convincing, ROI-focused; views Curaden as a business partner; wants to monetise learnings from courses; ambitious about clinic revenue and prestige

**Core challenge:** Wants better business models, pricing suggestions, and monetisation strategies. Finds Curaden's marketing support insufficient. Wants to be treated as a brand ambassador and receive special deals for high-volume ordering.

**What she wants from Curaden:** Business models and revenue streams beyond products; better marketing; practical help running and scaling a clinic; recognition as a high-volume partner

**iTOP relevance:** Medium-high. Motivated by business outcomes and patient loyalty that prevention generates. Needs to see the clinical model as commercially viable.

---

### Persona 3: The Involuntary Advisor
**Profile:** Dr. Julien Finch, 56, General dentist — large clinic, 12 employees

**Defining quote:** "I never wanted to be known as a dentist who is just fixing holes — this is not what I was born for."

**Key traits:** Micro-manager, process-oriented, likes things in order; recommends Curaprox products frequently; frustrated that patients call back to ask where/how to buy; wants partners who make his life easier

**Core challenge:** Over 60% of patients call back to ask about product purchasing. Too much brand confusion on the web (Curaden vs Curaprox). Wants patients to be more self-sufficient. Lacks post-visit material to leave with patients.

**What he wants from Curaden:** Less brand complexity; patient-facing educational content (online courses, better marketing materials); streamlined ordering and product discovery

**iTOP relevance:** High for scaled delivery. Already recommends the methodology — needs support turning that into patient-led behaviour change without requiring his personal time.

---

### Persona 4: The Prevention Opportunity Seeker
**Profile:** Lisa Contes, 28, Periodontist — works in traditional clinic with 6 employees, no decision-making power

**Defining quote:** "There are two kinds of professionals: those who just live to work and those who want to learn and improve."

**Key traits:** Driven, open to new approaches, motivated by prevention; lacks authority; blocked by superiors who don't see the business case; needs community and peer support

**Core challenge:** Cannot implement her prevention approach without convincing superiors of ROI. Needs a concrete business case and peer group to feel less isolated. Has no decision-making power at her clinic.

**What she wants from Curaden:** A business case to show superiors; coaching and sparring partner support after courses; peer community for exchange and continued learning; practical arguments for why prevention is commercially viable

**iTOP relevance:** Very high as a future advocate. Needs confidence, community, and support material — not more technique content.

---

### Persona 5: The Strategist
**Profile:** Dr. Peter Huber, 55, Initially dental surgeon now orthodontist — large perfectly designed clinic, 3 dentists, 5 assistants, 3 hygienists

**Defining quote:** "I think in the short term we spend more time with patients, but this is an investment into our image of preventive dental clinic: our unique selling point."

**Key traits:** Natural leader, charismatic, innovative, fast mover; uses iTOP as a clinic-wide system and USP; strategic and design-oriented; wife was the first in his clinic to do iTOP and drove the change

**Core challenge:** Initially sceptical of iTOP ("I had to admit I told my patients many wrong things before"). Overcame this to build an integrated prevention system. Some patients left — he's happy they did, because it clarified the clinic's positioning.

**What he wants from Curaden:** Standardised frameworks to scale prevention across a whole team; early access to new approaches; peer connections with other innovators

**iTOP relevance:** Highest value partner. Lighthouse practice potential. Fully integrated, clinic-wide implementation model.

---

### Persona 6: The Young Doc
**Profile:** Shin Bo, 25, Recently graduated, works in assistant role

**Defining quote:** "How dare you tell a 60-year-old patient how to brush their teeth?"

**Key traits:** Prevention is a core value; practical and hands-on learning oriented; empathetic; insecure about authority; under hierarchical pressure; visionary but vague

**Core challenge:** Frequently not taken seriously by patients or boss. Wants to implement prevention but faces resistance from superiors and older patients. Passionate about coaching model but lacks the authority and arguments to practise it.

**What she wants from Curaden:** Practical hands-on training; networking and community; techniques for building trust with patients across age groups; arguments for hierarchy; affordable pricing

**iTOP relevance:** High future value. Most likely to internalise the coaching model deeply if given support early in career.

---

### Persona 7: The Idealist
**Profile:** Dr. Mariela Stukova, 43, Part-time dentist — purpose-driven, holistic, long-term Curaden relationship

**Defining quote:** "My goal is being able to tell my patients how they can improve their overall health by taking care of their oral health."

**Key traits:** Altruistic, empathetic, holistic thinker; entire staff participates in iTOP courses; explores beyond Curaden (microbiome, hypnosis); conflict-avoidant; feels unique and isolated among peers

**Core challenge:** Tooth brushing is not taken seriously even by fellow dentists. Lonely in her approach. Worried about dentists who are completely ignorant toward proper brushing. Needs more information material from Curaden with new products.

**What she wants from Curaden:** Continued educational partnership and community; products with clear educational rationale; more focus on the whole-body connection in prevention content

**iTOP relevance:** Core loyal partner. High advocacy potential within her network, but network is small.

---

### Persona 8: The Friend
**Profile:** Dr. John Faller, 57, Endodontist — lone practitioner, many children in clinic, long Curaden history

**Defining quote:** "Now we use iTOP as a flagship for excellence — we are also the only ones in the area."

**Key traits:** Loyal, community-oriented, loves learning; participates in study groups; uses iTOP as differentiation vs larger clinics; frustrated by product availability and price inconsistency

**Core challenge:** Keeping patients motivated long-term. Competing with larger clinics. Some products not available in all countries. Product price concerns (quality vs "Made in China" label).

**What he wants from Curaden:** Continued support and community spirit; consistent product availability and quality signals; help with patient motivation tools

**iTOP relevance:** Strong advocate and community builder. Long-term, stable relationship. High Recall participation likelihood.

## Persona Prioritisation Framework

For each local market, Curaden teams should:

1. **Review all 8 personas** (quick summaries only)
2. **Map which appear most in your market** — note that not all 8 are always active
3. **Place each on a Value × Volume matrix**
4. **Select 2–3 priority personas** for the next 12 months

**Guiding questions:**
- Who drives the most value?
- Which personas are growing or strategically important?
- If we focused on only 2–3, which would make the biggest difference?

For each priority persona, localise only what truly differs from the global profile (goals, challenges, influencers, touchpoints, what they need from you).

## Personal Visit Guidelines

First contact should ideally be in-person. Visits should be selective — targeting clinics where personal contact adds value.

**Every personal visit presentation should include:**
- B2B portal and product access
- Full product portfolio
- iTOP programme and display opportunities

**Sales rep must have experienced iTOP personally** to convey its value authentically. Establish a FOMO effect to encourage participation.

**Visit frequency:** Agreed collaboratively with the dental professional. Suggested interval: every 2–3 months depending on engagement level.

**Visit checklist:**
- Identify target clinic and persona
- Schedule visit and confirm with client
- Prepare materials (portfolio, B2B portal guide, iTOP info)
- Conduct visit and record outcomes in CRM within 48 hours
- Schedule follow-up based on client needs

## CRM and Data Management

**System:** HubSpot or equivalent centralised CRM

**Data collection rules:**
- Primary collection at dental fairs and visits
- Enter all contact details into CRM within 48 hours of fair or visit
- Standardise categories: specialty, clinic type, engagement level, persona
- Segment contacts by persona and priority
- Plan personalised follow-up actions within 2 weeks of first contact

**Post-fair follow-up actions to plan:**
- Invitations to iTOP sessions
- Product presentations or demos
- Personalised emails or calls

## 5-Step Team Workshop Framework (Customer Journey)

For local teams (GM, Sales, Education, Marketing, iTOP) to align on customer journey priorities:

**Step 1 — Prioritise personas:** Value × Volume matrix → select 2–3 for the year

**Step 2 — Understand priority personas:** For each: goals, challenges, influencers, touchpoints, what they need from you

**Step 3 — Find critical moments:** Place each journey phase on Impact × Influence chart → choose 2–3 critical phases where improvement will have the biggest effect

**Step 4 — Turn insights into action:** For each critical moment: what's missing? What assets exist? What's new? Who owns it? What can be done in 1–3 months?

**Step 5 — Track and improve:** Choose 3–5 simple metrics, review monthly or quarterly

**Suggested tracking metrics:**
- Adoption of priority personas
- Usefulness of trainings (evaluation form rating)
- Conversion at key touchpoints
- Repeat purchases
- Engagement at critical phases
