# iTOP Protocols

Source: iTOP Guidelines 2024 + iTOP Implementation Introduction Brochure EN
Confidential — internal use only.

---

## §1: Philosophy & Three Pillars

**Core statement:** "Clean teeth cannot get sick and healthy gums do not bleed."

**Programme purpose:** Systematic, comprehensive, hands-on programme for daily biofilm management and establishment of good oral health habits.

**Three Pillars — every technique and tool must satisfy all three:**

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acceptable | Patient accepts the tools and methods into their daily routine |
| Non-traumatic | Techniques cause no damage to gums or dental tissue |
| Effective | Biofilm is actually removed when done correctly |

## §2: IAP/IAC Measurement Protocol

**Tools:** Interdental Access Probe (IAP) — colour-coded to match IDB brush sizes; Interdental Access Chart (IAC)

### Steps
1. Use the IAP to evaluate the correct IDB size for each individual interproximal space
2. Test validity by inserting the IDB of the equivalent colour into the space
3. Record findings on the IAC chart — mark with correct colour and a clear arrow showing insertion direction
4. Remeasure after 4–7 days if inflammation is present (spaces may enlarge as swelling subsides)

### Key Rules
- Each interdental space is different — always measure AND verify with IDB for each individual space
- Never assume size from adjacent spaces
- For fearful patients: begin measurement in lower lateral jaw (more spacious) to build confidence before continuing

## §3: Modified Bass Technique

Source: iTOP Guidelines 2024; developed by Charles Cassidy Bass

**Products:** CS 5460 ultra soft, CS smart

### Steps
1. Position brush half on gums and half on teeth, with bristles directed toward the sulcus area (slight angulation)
2. Once in the sulcus, release all pressure
3. Begin small circular movements, continuing along the jaw in overlapping strokes
4. Stay in the bordering area between soft and hard tissues — bristles always in the sulcus
5. Systematically clean all surfaces — no surface left untouched
6. Leave chewing surfaces until the end (exception: children)

### Key Rules
- Bristles must enter the sulcus — surface brushing is not sufficient
- Zero pressure once in sulcus — movement does the work, not force
- Systematic zone coverage is mandatory — jumping between zones produces inconsistent habit formation
- Start from the area most often missed — builds thorough habits

### Common Errors
- Pressing too hard: traumatises gingiva, does not improve cleaning
- Missing the sulcus: surface brushing leaves biofilm at the critical gum line
- Skipping zones: produces incomplete biofilm removal

### Tips for Instructors
- Ask patient to feel the difference between surface brushing and sulcus placement — proprioceptive awareness is the teaching moment
- Deep fissures of newly grown permanent dentition require particular attention

## §4: IDB (Interdental Brush) Technique

Source: iTOP Guidelines 2024 + Introduction Brochure

**Products:** CPS prime (06, 07, 08, 09, 011), CPS perio (405, 406, 408, 410)

Interdental spaces = approximately 30% of tooth surfaces. Interdental brushes are superior to floss in biofilm removal for most spaces.

### Steps
1. Measure space with IAP (§2 above) and record on IAC chart
2. Select IDB of the correct size for each space
3. Insert the IDB gently into the interproximal space — no forcing
4. Use short back-and-forth strokes in the space
5. Umbrella effect of bristles cleans all critical areas between teeth
6. Move systematically through all measured spaces

### Key Rules
- Correct size is essential — wrong size either fails to clean or traumatises tissue
- Each space may require a different size
- Begin with maximum two different IDB sizes for new patients — do not overwhelm
- Primary goal: remain non-traumatic so the patient adopts IDB use daily

### Tips for Instructors
- Tip 1: For patients with inflammation — remind them healthy gums do not bleed. IDB use will reduce bleeding within days if done correctly
- Tip 2: Remeasure all spaces 4–7 days after initial instruction as inflammation subsides
- Tip 3: Acceptability is much higher than dental floss — emphasise this to hesitant patients

## §5: Solo Technique

Source: iTOP Introduction Brochure

**Product:** CS 1006 single brush (developed by Jiri Sedelmayer for this technique); CS 1009

### Steps
1. No toothpaste or water needed — this technique relies entirely on touch feedback
2. Take a comfortable position — not necessarily in front of a mirror after initial orientation
3. Place bristles directly in the sulcus, on hard dental tissue only
4. Clean tooth by tooth — each tooth individually
5. Do not skip chewing surfaces, especially deep fissures

### Key Rules
- Bristles stay only on hard dental surfaces, under gums, directly in sulcus
- No chemical aid — pure tactile technique
- No more effective approach than tooth-by-tooth cleaning exists

### Tips for Instructors
- Tip 1: Encourage patients to practise whenever convenient — on train, watching TV, reading. Removes the time-pressure barrier
- Tip 2: Particularly valuable for patients with braces and implants
- Tip 3: Acceptability is high once learned — emphasise the feeling of cleanliness

## §6: Loop Floss Technique

Source: iTOP Introduction Brochure

**Product:** DF 834 dental floss

**Indication:** Front teeth only — tightly packed spaces where no IDB can fit, and on long parallel tooth surfaces at incisors. Not a general substitute for IDB.

**Important:** Using floss incorrectly — stretched tautly across concave surfaces — can cut deep into gum and cause severe trauma. Only well-trained professionals should instruct this technique.

### Steps
1. Make a loop that fits closely over three or four fingers
2. With a gentle sawing motion, cross the contact point — the loop provides safe, controlled movement
3. Once past the contact point, curve floss around one tooth surface and move up and down
4. Repeat on the adjacent tooth surface before sliding out
5. Repeat two or three times per space

### Key Rules
- Loop gives control — never use floss without the loop
- Do not slide out between strokes — complete both surfaces before exiting the space
- Movement is similar to deep scaling with curettes — controlled, deliberate
- If patient cannot achieve correct technique: remain with IDB

### Tips for Instructors
- Tip 1: Take time with the loop — practice the hand position before attempting in the mouth
- Tip 2: Acceptability is low — only instruct for front teeth and where IDB cannot access
- Tip 3: If compliance is poor, IDB remains more effective than incorrectly used floss

## §7: Session Structure

### Initial Session
1. Assess patient — identify all interdental spaces requiring IDB
2. IAP measurement and IAC charting (§2)
3. Select and introduce maximum 2 IDB sizes initially
4. Demonstrate Bass technique on typodont (T2T) before patient application
5. Guided patient practice — touch-correct-practise sequence
6. Introduce solo technique if appropriate
7. Loop floss technique for front teeth only if indicated
8. Assign home practice with specific daily targets

### Follow-Up Session
1. Review IAC chart and BOB scores
2. Remeasure any spaces where inflammation has subsided
3. Assess home practice compliance and identify barriers
4. Targeted T2T correction on weak areas only
5. Progress to next skill or additional IDB sizes if ready

### Re-Check Session
1. Full BOB measurement
2. Adjust IDB sizes if gum health has improved
3. Motivational interviewing — celebrate measurable improvement
4. Long-term maintenance planning

## §8: Patient Coaching Principles

- The clinician's role is that of a **coach** — patient and gradual
- Success is the best motivation: no more cavities, no more bleeding
- Start slowly — the patient is breaking and changing an existing habit
- Every patient (healthy or with gingivitis/periodontitis) needs interdental brushes
- One-on-one instruction is the ideal format
- Dentist and team must share the same philosophy — unified message builds patient trust
- Good oral health directly correlates to overall general health
