# Educational Psychology Principles

Applied to iTOP lesson plans, SOPs, and feedback synthesis. These principles inform how training is designed — they do not override clinical protocols.

---

## 1. Spaced Repetition

**Principle:** Learning is strengthened when practice is distributed over time rather than massed in a single session.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Every lesson plan must schedule a revisit of the previous session's key skill
- Competency gates are checked at the start of the following session, not just at the end of the current one
- Recommended intervals: 1 week → 2 weeks → 1 month → 3 months for new technique retention

**Instructor prompt:** "Before we start today — let's quickly revisit the Bass technique from last time."

---

## 2. Cognitive Load Theory

**Principle:** Working memory is limited. Overloading it with too much new information at once impairs learning.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Theory blocks capped at 15 minutes before a hands-on anchor
- Introduce one micro-skill at a time (e.g., angulation before pressure before stroke)
- Use physical demonstration to offload cognitive processing — the body learns while the mind follows
- Avoid delivering protocol rationale before the learner has experienced the technique

**Instructor prompt:** "Don't think about the theory yet — just feel what happens when you change the angle."

---

## 3. Deliberate Practice

**Principle:** Skill improvement comes from focused, effortful practice on specific weaknesses — not from repeating what is already comfortable.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Each Practice block in a lesson plan must name the specific micro-skill being practised
- Instructor identifies and targets the learner's weakest zone or movement — not the whole technique
- Feedback is specific and immediate: "Your zone 3 angulation dropped — let's do just zone 3 again"
- Comfort does not equal competency

**Instructor prompt:** "You've got zones 1–2 well. Let's spend the next 10 minutes on zone 4 specifically."

---

## 4. Motivational Interviewing (MI) in Clinical Education

**Principle:** Adults learn better when they connect the skill to their own values and patient outcomes — not when told to comply.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Open the session with a patient story, not a protocol statement
- Ask "What difference do you think this would make for your patients?" before explaining the technique
- Reflect back the learner's own words: "You said your patients struggle with compliance — how does this technique address that?"
- Avoid directive language in feedback; use exploratory language

**Instructor prompt:** "What are you noticing in your patients' BOB scores when you apply this consistently?"

---

## 5. Adult Learning Theory (Andragogy)

**Principle:** Adult learners are self-directed, experience-driven, and need to understand the relevance of what they are learning.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Connect every technique to a clinical outcome the practitioner cares about
- Respect existing clinical experience — frame iTOP as a refinement, not a replacement
- Give learners agency in the feedback phase: "What do you think you'd change?"
- Avoid patronising language; frame assessments as professional development, not testing

---

## 6. Behaviour Change Models

**Principle:** Changing clinical habits requires more than knowledge — it requires motivation, environmental cues, and reinforced practice.

**Application in iTOP training:**
- Identify which stage a learner is at: Pre-contemplation / Contemplation / Preparation / Action / Maintenance
- Lesson plans for students in Action or Maintenance stage emphasise refinement, not re-motivation
- Build environmental prompts into SOPs: "Post BOB scoring chart at chairside"
- Celebrate small measurable improvements (BOB% reduction) as behaviour reinforcement

---

## 7. COM-B Model — Patient Behaviour Change

> Source: Barbara Olejarová / Mario guidelines collaboration, April 2025
> **Critical insight:** Traditional iTOP + BOB methodology assumes *explain → show data → patient acts*. Behavioural science evidence shows knowledge alone does not sustain behaviour. iTOP must shift from information delivery to behaviour architecture.

**Principle:** Behaviour only occurs when Capability, Opportunity, and Motivation are all present simultaneously. If any one is missing, the behaviour will not be adopted or maintained — regardless of how well the clinical instruction was delivered.

| Component | Question to ask | iTOP application |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Capability** | Can the patient physically perform the technique? Do they have the right tool? | T2T is strong here — but capability must extend *beyond the chair* into the home routine |
| **Opportunity** | Does their daily environment support the behaviour? Is the brush visible, accessible, timed? | Currently absent from iTOP methodology — must be explicitly addressed |
| **Motivation** | Is the goal meaningful to *this patient*, in *their* frame? | Requires individual elicitation — not a standard motivational pitch |

**Application in iTOP patient sessions:**
- Before any clinical instruction, ask one open question about what the patient wants for their own health — not what the clinician wants for them
- Identify which COM-B component is the weakest barrier for this patient and address that first
- Never assume motivation is present because capability has been taught

**Instructor prompt (for training clinicians):** "Which of the three — capability, opportunity, or motivation — do you think is the actual barrier for this patient?"

## 8. Micro Goals and Implementation Intentions

**Principle:** Specific, small, time-anchored action plans produce significantly higher follow-through than general instructions.

**The problem with current iTOP patient instruction:**
Asking a patient to begin using IDBs in all interdental spaces immediately is a behaviour change cliff. The instruction is technically correct but behaviourally unrealistic as a starting point.

**Application — the one-thing rule:**
Every patient session must close with one specific implementation intention, not a full protocol instruction:
- Specific tool: *"the pink brush"*
- Specific location: *"the two gaps at the lower front"*
- Specific time anchor: *"after you brush tonight"*
- Expected sensation: *"it may feel tight — that's correct, don't force it"*

Example: *"After you brush tonight, use the pink brush in the two lower front gaps. It will take about 30 seconds."*

This is plannable, visualisable, and achievable — and creates the foundation for the follow-up conversation.

**Instructor prompt (for training clinicians):** "Don't send them home with the full chart. Send them home with one thing they can actually do tonight."

---

## 9. Follow-Up as the Behaviour Change Mechanism

**Principle:** Follow-up is not administrative — it is where most patient behaviour change actually occurs or fails. It must be structured as a coaching conversation, not a data review.

**Current iTOP gap:**
Follow-up is currently positioned in the *After* section of seminar planning as a sales rep action (1–2 weeks post-seminar). This frames follow-up as commercial activity. For patient behaviour change, follow-up must be reframed as the primary reinforcement loop.

**Follow-up conversation structure (for clinicians to use with patients):**
1. **Check the plan** — "Did you try the pink brush in those two gaps?" (not "how is your oral hygiene?")
2. **Celebrate or troubleshoot** — if yes: acknowledge specifically. If no: explore the obstacle without judgement
3. **Adjust** — make the plan easier if needed, or extend to one additional space if ready
4. **Re-anchor** — close with the next specific micro goal

**BOB scores in follow-up — reframed for patients:**
BOB% is a clinician metric. Patients do not experience percentages — they experience *"my gums stopped bleeding"* or *"it only takes me two minutes now."*
- Lead with the patient's experience, not the number
- Use the number to confirm and celebrate: *"Your score went from 23 to 11 — that's what two minutes every evening looks like over three weeks."*
- Never open a follow-up by showing a chart before asking how it felt

**Instructor prompt:** "The score is yours to track. The story belongs to the patient — let them tell it first."
