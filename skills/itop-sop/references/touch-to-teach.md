# Touch-to-Teach Framework

Core hands-on teaching philosophy for iTOP training. Inferred from iTOP methodology and Curaden Academy practice principles.

Technology is a **future enhancement layer** — current training is entirely hands-on and tactile.

---

## 7 Core Principles

### 1. Tactile Before Cognitive
Physical demonstration precedes verbal explanation. Participants feel correct technique before they hear about it. Theory is always anchored to a physical experience, never delivered in isolation.

### 2. Proprioceptive Learning
The instructor physically guides the learner's hand — adjusting grip, angulation, pressure, and stroke. The goal is for the learner to internalise the correct sensation of the technique, not just observe it. Touching is the teaching.

### 3. ODPF Cycle (Observe → Demonstrate → Practice → Feedback)
Every skill block follows this sequence:
- **O — Observe:** Learner watches instructor perform on typodont; no instruments in learner hands
- **D — Demonstrate (guided):** Instructor demonstrates step-by-step with narration; learner mirrors
- **P — Practice:** Learner practises on typodont; instructor coaches in real time
- **F — Feedback:** Structured feedback on observable criteria; not impressionistic

Never skip or reorder steps. Skipping Observe and going straight to Practice is the most common instructor error.

### 4. Zone-by-Zone Systematic Application
Technique is always taught and practised zone by zone (e.g., upper right buccal → upper anterior → upper left buccal → lower right buccal → lower anterior → lower left buccal). Systematic = repeatable = learnable. Jumping between zones produces inconsistent habit formation.

### 5. Instructor as Coach
The instructor does not lecture. The instructor watches, corrects, encourages, and adjusts. Ratio: ≤20% talking, ≥80% observing and physically coaching. Celebrate micro-improvements explicitly.

### 6. Typodont-First Rule
No learner performs a new technique on a patient before achieving competency on a typodont/phantom model. There are no exceptions. Clinical introduction follows demonstrated model competency.

### 7. Technology as Future Enhancer
Tools such as the BOB App, intraoral cameras, and digital tracking augment the feedback loop — they do not replace physical coaching. Current iTOP training is fully hands-on. Technology integration is a planned future layer. Do not build lesson plans that depend on technology.

## ODPF Time Ratios

| Session Duration | Observe | Demonstrate | Practice | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 5 min | 5 min | 15 min | 5 min |
| 60 min | 8 min | 10 min | 32 min | 10 min |
| 90 min | 10 min | 15 min | 50 min | 15 min |
| 120 min | 10 min | 20 min | 70 min | 20 min |

Practical time (Practice + partial Feedback) must be ≥60% of total session time.

---

## Common Instructor Errors to Avoid

| Error | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Lecturing before demonstrating | Cognitive load without physical anchor — poor retention |
| Skipping Observe phase | Learners form incorrect movement patterns from the start |
| Not physically correcting grip | Learner ingrains wrong proprioceptive memory |
| Moving to next zone before competency checkpoint | Inconsistent technique across zones |
| Introducing patient before typodont competency | Clinical risk; destroys learner confidence |
| Using technology as a substitute for hands-on coaching | Disconnects tactile learning from feedback |

## Competency Checkpoint Format

At the end of each Practice block, apply:

```
COMPETENCY CHECKPOINT
  Skill: {specific technique or zone}
  Observable pass criterion: {exactly what the instructor sees/feels}
  Pass: proceed to next zone / next session
  Not yet: {specific corrective action} → re-practice → re-check
```
